Day 1

The Unboxing

"How hard can it be?"

Expected

A kit. Some assembly. A couple of USB cables. Done.

Reality

200+ parts. One warped 3D-printed piece. Two wrong servo motors. A firmware tool that only runs on Windows.

The SO-101 kit is open-source hardware at its best and worst.
Best: you can fix anything. Worst: you will need to fix things.

I'm on Fedora. The motor firmware update tool is a Windows .exe.
I briefly considered installing Windows. That's how desperate I was.

Day 3

It Moves!

The leader arm moves. The follower arm follows.

You move your wrist and a machine six inches away mirrors you in real time. It is, genuinely, magical.

And then...

  • Movements are jerky — some servos lag behind
  • USB cameras disconnect randomly after 10 minutes
  • Recording software crashes on Wayland
  • Display output drops camera FPS from 30 to 8

Welcome to embedded Linux.

Week 1

Recording Episodes

50 attempts to grab a red block. Each recording session:

  1. Check cameras — USB ports shuffle on every reboot. /dev/video0 is now /dev/video4. Why? Nobody knows.
  2. Check FPS — too slow if a display is connected. Run headless.
  3. Check recording — are we capturing both action AND observation? Both cameras? Is the data actually being written to disk?
  4. Perform the grab — 15 seconds of careful teleoperation
  5. Verify the episode — replay the data. Was it clean?

Lesson #1: Data collection IS the job.
Not a step before the job. Not a prerequisite. The job.

Week 2

"Just Train and Deploy"

Training

ACT policy. 1 hour on GPU.
Loss: 0.08
Looks great.

Deployment

Robot reaches for the block...
and misses.
Every. Single. Time.

Not randomly. Systematically. Always 6-7 cm to the right.

Maybe the model isn't big enough?
Try ACT → misses → Try Pi0.5 → misses → Try SmolVLA → misses

The problem isn't the model.

The Investigation

Something Is Wrong

Clue #1
$ nvidia-smi
GPU utilization: 2%

# The policy was running on CPU.
# Nobody told me.

Adding --policy.device=cuda fixes the speed.
But the robot still misses.

Clue #2

I add profiling. Each control loop: 5ms. Policy inference: fast.
Camera capture: fine. Motor commands: sending correctly.
Everything looks perfect. The robot should work.

WHY doesn't it work?

The Answer

Wrist motor offset between leader and follower

17.5°

Every single episode I recorded taught the robot
a physically incorrect mapping of the world.

A 40-line Python script found in 3 seconds what I couldn't find in 2 weeks.

0%
Before recalibration
→
80%
After recalibration

Same policy. Same code. Same hyperparameters.

The Journey

What It Actually Felt Like

Reflections

What I Learned About Physical AI

1. Hardware fights back.
Defective parts, firmware quirks, calibration drift. Software has bugs. Hardware has bugs that are also physical objects.

2. "The data looks right" doesn't mean "the setup is right."
My data was perfectly formatted, correctly structured, valid in every way. It was also teaching the robot a wrong model of reality.

3. Loss curves lie.
0.08 loss with a 17.5° calibration error = 6cm of real-world error. The math was perfect. The physics was wrong.

4. Every shortcut becomes a 10x debugging session.
Skip calibration verification? Enjoy two weeks of "why doesn't this work?"

5. The gap between paper and robot is ALWAYS wider than you think.
Papers describe the destination. They rarely describe the journey.

The Turning Point

The Tools That Saved Me

Simple Python scripts. ~600 lines total. Changed debugging from
"I have intuitions" to "I have data."

compare_leader_follower.py

Compares leader arm positions against follower positions in recorded episodes.

Found the 17.5° offset in 3 seconds.

evaluate_dataset_quality.py

Checks for outlier episodes, inconsistent timing, camera drops, and motor anomalies.

Flagged 11 bad episodes automatically.

Sometimes the most impactful code you write
is the diagnostic code.

The Payoff

It Works.

90 seconds of a robot arm that finally grabs things.

It's not perfect. It's real.

Advice

For the Brave

1

Budget 5–10x your optimistic estimate.
If you think it's a weekend, plan for a month.

2

Invest in diagnostics BEFORE bigger models.
A 40-line script beat three state-of-the-art neural networks.

3

Physical calibration > ML hyperparameters.
No learning rate will fix a 17.5° offset.

4

Check assumptions with data, not intuition.
Your gut feeling about why it's failing is probably wrong.

5

The robot doesn't care about your loss curve.
It cares about physical reality. And physical reality always wins.

"Robot learning is beautiful because it forces you to confront reality.

A mathematically perfect model that fails in practice
teaches you more than any abstract benchmark."
